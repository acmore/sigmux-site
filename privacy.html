Sigmux

Privacy Policy

Effective October 5, 2026
Sigmux is an SSH terminal for iOS. It is built so that your data stays on your device: no accounts, no analytics, no tracking, and no servers of ours in between you and your machines. The one exception is optional push notifications, which pass through a relay we run that cannot read them.

Data we collect

Sigmux has no analytics, advertising, or tracking SDKs, and no user accounts. If you turn on Sigmux notifications (Settings → Agents), the app registers your phone with our push relay; what the relay keeps is listed under Push notifications below. Nothing else is collected, transmitted, stored, or sold.

Data stored on your device

  • Host profiles — hostnames, ports, usernames, and connection settings are stored locally on your device only.
  • Credentials — SSH private keys, passwords, and passphrases are stored exclusively in the iOS Keychain with device-only accessibility. They are never synced to iCloud and never leave your device.
  • Terminal content — scrollback and session output are held in memory only and are not persisted to disk.
  • Compose drafts — an unsent note per host is saved on your device so it survives a restart. It is excluded from backups.
  • Diagnostics — crash and hang reports from iOS (MetricKit), and connection traces you turn on in Settings, are written to the app's Documents folder on your device. They are never uploaded unless you share the files yourself.

Network connections

Sigmux connects directly from your device to the SSH servers you configure. Your terminal traffic flows only between your device and your server, encrypted end to end by the SSH protocol; no server of ours is in between. The only connection to us is the push relay described next, and only when you turn notifications on.

Push notifications

Hosts can notify your phone when a coding agent finishes or waits for your approval. With Sigmux notifications, each host encrypts every notification with keys that exist only on that host and on your phone, and sends the sealed message to our relay (push.acmore.dev, a Cloudflare Worker with a database in the Asia-Pacific region). The relay signs it for Apple Push Notification service and forwards it; Sigmux decrypts it on your phone.

  • What the relay stores — your phone's Apple push token and whether it is a beta or App Store install; for each host you set up, a random key identifier and the SHA-256 hash of that host's send token; and per-host counters used for rate limiting. Secrets are stored only as hashes.
  • What the relay never sees — the notification's text, the host's name or address, your session or window names, the agent's words, or the commands it asks about. It cannot decrypt what it forwards.
  • What Apple sees — Apple delivers the still-sealed message to your phone and does not have the keys to read it.
  • Answering from a notification — when you tap Allow or Deny on a notification, Sigmux connects from your phone to that host over SSH, exactly as it would in the app, and sends your answer. Nothing about your answer goes through the relay.
  • Stopping — removing Sigmux notifications from a host (Settings → Agents) deletes that host's keys on the relay and on your phone. Deleting the app invalidates your push token, and the relay discards it the next time a host tries to send. You can also ask us to delete your registration at the address below.

If you choose Bark, ntfy, or a webhook instead, hosts send notifications straight to that service under its own privacy policy; our relay is not involved. The relay's source code is published with the app's repository.

Face ID and Touch ID

Biometric authentication is handled entirely on-device by iOS. Sigmux never receives, stores, or transmits biometric data; it only learns whether the system reported the check as passed.

Beta builds (TestFlight)

If you use a beta version of Sigmux through TestFlight, Apple may collect crash logs, usage statistics, and feedback you choose to submit, and share them with us under Apple's TestFlight terms. We use this information solely to diagnose and fix bugs.
